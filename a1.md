# Introduction

The renovation of Building 308 in the client report from group 9 includes changes to the building, including the addition of two new floors and changes to the internal layout of the building. Changes like these affect existing structural system and need to be investigated. The aim of the tool we have chosen, here being tool 2548 is to use information from the IFC model to support an early structural assessment of the proposed design that was given in the client report.

## identified problem
we have chosen to look at two main structural issues from the report.

The first issue is the addition of two new floors. The new floors increase the self weight and loads on the beams, columns, and foundations. Some structural elements already have relatively high utilization, which means the additional loading could become critical for the building.

The second issue we looked at from the report is the removal of internal walls to create larger and more open spaces. Some walls may be important  since they are load bearing. Removing these walls could therefore affect how forces are transferred through the existing structure.

## project claim
IfcOpenShell can be used to extract information from the IFC model to identify and estimate the structural impact of the two problems we have chosen to look at, here being the vertical extension and wall removals.

## Tool 2548
Tool 2548 is originally used to calculate building areas and estimate rough costs from an IFC model. It uses IfcOpenShell to find elements such as IfcSpace and IfcWall and extracts quantities such as floor area, wall area, and volume. These quantities are then combined with prices from a CSV price list to calculate an approximate building cost.

For our project, we want to keep the original cost estimation and expand the tool with structural checks. The same IFC information can therefore be used for two purposes.

For the new floors, the extracted floor area can be used to calculate both the approximate cost and tell if its load bearing or not. For walls, the tool can still use their quantities for the cost estimate, while also checking available IFC properties to identify whether walls planned for removal are marked as load-bearing under the quantity set.

The adapted tool would therefore work like this:

The tool first reads the IFC model and extracts information about the spaces and walls, including their areas and other available under quantities. The areas can be combined with a price list to estimate the cost of the renovation, while the areas of the new floors can be combined with structural loads to estimate how much additional load they add to the existing building. At the same time, the tool can check the properties of walls that are planned for removal and flag them if they are identified as load-bearing.

This allows one tool to provide information about both the economic and structural impact of the proposed renovation.

## Proposed Adaptation of the Tool
We will keep the original functions of Tool 2548, such as extracting areas from the IFC model and using them for rough cost estimation. The main purpose of the tool will therefore remain the same.

In addition, we will use the IFC properties connected to the building elements to identify possible structural issues. For example, when the tool identifies an IfcWall, it can check the available properties of that wall. If a wall affected by the renovation is marked as load-bearing, the tool can display a warning that the wall requires structural investigation before it is removed.

The idea is not to calculate the structural capacity of the wall or perform a full structural analysis. Instead, we use information that already exists in the IFC model to highlight potential problems. This allows Tool 2548 to provide both its original area and cost information and simple structural warnings that can support the renovation process.
